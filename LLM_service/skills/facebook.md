# Facebook — platform style skill

The creator reads this file and injects it as the static style layer when drafting a
Facebook post (MIGRATION_PLAN §5.4); the roundtable's `platform_editor` seat reads the
same file. Edit it to retune the platform's house style.

## Format
- Character limit: 63206
- Sweet spot: 40–250 characters. Short, conversational posts get the most reach; the
  first ~250 characters show before "See more", so land the point up front.
- 1–3 short paragraphs with generous line breaks — the feed rewards a friendly,
  skimmable rhythm over dense blocks.
- Links go **in the body** (Facebook renders a rich preview card — unlike LinkedIn).
  0–2 hashtags at most; they matter far less here than on Instagram or X.
- Native photo/video and a genuine question outperform bare link-drops.

## Anatomy of a strong post
1. **Hook** (line 1): a relatable moment, a bit of news, or a question — the part above
   "See more".
2. **The story** (1–2 short paragraphs): the human beat that makes it worth a share.
3. **Conversation starter** (last line): a genuine question or a reason to tag a friend —
   Facebook's algorithm rewards comments and shares over passive likes.

## Tone
- Warm, community-first, conversational — write like a neighbour sharing news, not a
  press release. Facebook's audience skews toward connection and shareable stories.
- Open a loop that invites comments and shares.
- Emojis as warmth, not decoration — a few, not a wall.

## Openers that work
- "[N] years ago today, we [humble beginning]…"
- "Real talk: [honest, relatable admission]."
- "We've got news — and we wanted you to hear it here first."

## Calls to action that work
- "What was your first [experience with us]? 👇" · "Tag someone who'd love this."
- "Tell us your story in the comments." — ask a real question, don't beg for likes.

## Do / Don't
- ✅ Conversational hook; a shareable human story; a genuine comment-driving question.
- ✅ Link in the body (with its preview); 0–2 hashtags; a few warm emojis.
- ❌ Press-release voice, "link in bio" (that's Instagram — put the link here), hashtag
  stuffing, or a post that asks nothing of the reader.

## Good example
> We roasted our very first batch in a rented garage six years ago today. ☕️
>
> This morning we shipped our 100,000th bag — same beans, same obsessive attention,
> a slightly bigger garage.
>
> Thank you for growing this with us. What was the first bag you ever ordered from us? 👇

## Bad example (avoid)
> ANNOUNCEMENT!!! Our new coffee is available now, click the link in our bio to purchase
> today, limited time only!!! #coffee #coffee #sale #buynow #love #instagood #follow

Why it's bad: shouty press-release voice, "link in bio" (that's Instagram — put the link
here), hashtag stuffing, and it asks nothing of the reader, so nobody comments or shares.

## Self-check before posting
- Is the hook conversational and human, not a press release?
- Is there a genuine question or tag prompt that would actually earn a comment?
- Link in the body (not "in bio"), 0–2 hashtags, short and skimmable?
