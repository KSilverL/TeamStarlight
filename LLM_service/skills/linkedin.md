# LinkedIn — platform style skill

The creator reads this file and injects it as the static style layer when drafting a
LinkedIn post (MIGRATION_PLAN §5.4); the roundtable's `platform_editor` seat reads the
same file. Edit it to retune the platform's house style.

## Format
- Character limit: 3000
- Sweet spot: 900–1300 characters. Only the first ~2 lines (~140 characters) show before
  "…see more", so the hook must earn the expand on its own.
- Short paragraphs (1–3 lines) with a blank line between them. No walls of text.
- 3–5 focused CamelCase hashtags at the very end (`#SpecialtyCoffee`, not `#specialty`).
- No link in the body — it suppresses reach. Put the URL in the first comment and say so.
- Native text, documents (carousels), and polls outrank bare outbound links.

## Anatomy of a strong post
1. **Hook** (line 1–2): a data point, a contrarian claim, or the opening beat of a story —
   this is all that shows before the fold, so it must stand alone.
2. **Substance** (2–4 short paragraphs): the actual insight. Show the reasoning, name the
   trade-off, admit what you got wrong. Specific beats sweeping every time.
3. **Takeaway + question** (last 2 lines): one line that lands the point, then an open
   question that invites practitioners to reply.

## Tone
- Professional but human: insightful, credible, first-person, specific.
- Lead with a number, a strong opinion, or a concrete moment — never "We're excited to
  announce".
- Teach or reveal something: leave the reader a little smarter for having read it.

## Openers that work
- "Most [X] advice is wrong. Here's what the numbers actually say."
- "We [made a costly mistake]. Here's what it cost us — and what it taught us."
- "[Surprising figure]. That's [context], and it changed how we [do X]."

## Calls to action that work
- "How does your team handle this?" · "What would you add?" · "Does this match your experience?"
- Invite a reply, not a metric — skip "like and share".

## Do / Don't
- ✅ One clear idea argued with specifics; first-person voice; a genuine closing question.
- ✅ Hashtags at the end; the link lives in the first comment.
- ❌ Hype, exclamation spam, buzzword soup, hashtag stuffing, or a link dropped in the body.

## Good example
> Most "sustainability" claims in coffee are vibes, not numbers.
>
> So this season we published ours: 1,200 farmers, 38 hectares, one harvest window.
>
> Here's what the data changed about how we roast — and what we got wrong last year. 👇
>
> #SpecialtyCoffee #Sustainability #SupplyChain

## Bad example (avoid)
> 🚀🚀 We are SUPER EXCITED to ANNOUNCE our amazing new product!!! Check the link!!!
> #coffee #coffee #coffee #coffee #coffee #love #instagood #follow #like

Why it's bad: hype with no substance, exclamation spam, link in body, hashtag stuffing,
no point of view.

## Self-check before posting
- Does line 1 stand alone and make someone tap "see more"?
- Is there a specific number, story, or opinion — not just an announcement?
- Under ~1300 characters, 3–5 hashtags, link in the first comment, closes with a question?
