# Instagram — platform style skill

The creator reads this file and injects it as the static style layer when drafting an
Instagram caption (MIGRATION_PLAN §5.4); the roundtable's `platform_editor` seat reads
the same file. Edit it to retune the house style.

## Format
- Character limit: 2200
- Sweet spot: 125–400 characters before the "…more" fold — make the first line a hook.
- The caption is paired with a strong visual; the words add story, they don't describe
  the photo. Assume the image already carries the "what".
- Emojis as visual rhythm (line starts, bullets) — a few, never a wall.
- 3–8 hashtags, mixing broad + niche, on their own line or in the first comment.
- One CTA only, and Instagram's real link lives in bio / link sticker — say "link in bio".

## Anatomy of a strong caption
1. **Hook** (line 1): a feeling, a question, or a bold line — the part above "…more".
2. **Story** (2–4 short lines): the sensory or emotional beat the photo can't say in words.
3. **Prompt + CTA** (last lines): invite a save, share, or tag, then point to the link.

## Tone
- Warm, lifestyle, sensory, aspirational. Show the feeling, not the spec sheet.
- Invite the save/share: "tag someone who…", "save this for…".
- A clear, friendly CTA (link in bio) — one, not three.

## Openers that work
- "POV: [the moment your product creates]."
- "The one thing nobody tells you about [topic]…"
- "Save this for [specific future moment]."

## Calls to action that work
- "📌 Save this for [occasion]." · "Tag someone who [fits]." · "Which one's yours? 👇"
- "🔗 Link in bio" — never a raw URL (it isn't clickable in-caption).

## Do / Don't
- ✅ First-line hook; sensory story; a save/share/tag prompt; emojis as rhythm.
- ✅ 3–8 relevant hashtags; one CTA; lean on the visual for the literal facts.
- ❌ Describing the photo, transactional "buy now" flatness, hashtag dumps, pasted URLs.

## Good example
> ✨ Harvest, in a cup.
>
> This Ethiopian single-origin tastes like jasmine and stone fruit — picked at 1,900m,
> roasted light to keep every floral note.
>
> 📌 Save this for your next slow morning.
> 🔗 Limited drop — link in bio.
>
> #SpecialtyCoffee #SingleOrigin #SlowMornings #CoffeeLovers

## Bad example (avoid)
> Buy our coffee. It is good coffee. Available now. #coffee

Why it's bad: flat and transactional, no sensory story, no hook, no save/share prompt,
wastes the visual-first format.

## Self-check before posting
- Does line 1 hook without describing the image?
- Is there a sensory or emotional beat the photo alone can't deliver?
- One CTA, "link in bio" (no raw URL), 3–8 relevant hashtags, a few emojis as rhythm?
